Olivier, granted me an interview. To sum up his career: water and sanitation and civil engineering, Congo (displaced population), Ethiopia, Uganda. Work with Solidarité International. He is now CEO of Baobab Tech.

I asked him a few questions:
How did you get in the humanitarian world? I started off in Belgium when I was 16, working on micro-barrages in West Africa. I did some unpaid internship after traveling alone into Uganda. Humanitarian work in water supply in Ethiopia. Internships (in Tunisia), understanding the dev sector, paid internship managing staff in Ethiopia, Congo with Solidarité, Sudan Irish NGO, international expats.

Organisations hire national staff now, it is very different to get to this type of positions in WASH. 
I studied a year in Canada, and I  was looking for organisations that are building support and capabilites abroad instead of working there. It made more sense to be a trainer, I was part of the executive team, managing half of the organisation.
I am now working in Calgary, and slowed down on travel with kids, 10 and 13, but I worked in Cameroun and  Zambia, as well.

About Baobab tech?
Our sector is broader social environement impact space, for profit space that started with the emergence of Generative AI and language models. I started off before GPT3 came out.
In the water sector you can use knowledge retrieval systems, GenAI, there is a lot of unstructured data, big pools of data and it is hard to find people who know to work well in this space. We are not building ML models, we work on more generative AI and ML / NLP spaces.
We work mostly contract-based. Not a lot of people can do that and it is all new on several grounds: products tools, being within this social environement space and in the water space. People should be able to combine unstructured knowledge retrieval, climate related data, etc. We need some help in data science, we hire developpers, web app dev and it is project-based. The company, even for profit, has a slow organic growth. We are trying to stay very lean.

What happens in Africa?  We might be partenering with some organisations. There exists digital solutions, for instance in Nigeria which is quite vibrant with technical solutions and AI powered retrieval for water and sanitation. There are geospecific capabilities, build in countries or improved in countries, even regionally, in East Africa.

What is your view on using google partnerships?
Social Impact interest in big corporations in general is kind of the same, for instance at Microsoft, which is the preferred framework for NGOs, and they propose combination of services: grant on ideas, pair up with big NGOs, work with Save the Children, pairing engineers with them
Google works on large scale projects that can become quite local, for instance, they have expanded flood alerts tools to 100 countries.
